# Party and social games

Local or online games played together for social fun: minigame collections, drawing and
trivia party packs, couch co-op chaos (cooking, moving furniture), social deduction, and
cozy social spaces.

> **Sourcing note:** no primary party-game postmortem was retrieved. Guidance draws on
> Lazzaro's *People Fun*, MDA's *Fellowship*, social design in Journey, and general practice.
> Playtest with real groups. Party games are about the group's experience.

## Core loop
Gather (join quickly) → a short round with a simple rule → a funny or dramatic outcome
(often shared failure) → reactions (laughter, accusation, banter) → scores or roles shift →
the next round.

## Signature mechanics
- Very low entry: join with a phone, one-button controls, rules explained in seconds.
- **Social emotions** as the payload: teasing, betrayal, cooperation, performance.
- Asymmetric roles and hidden information (social deduction).
- Chaos and physical comedy (cooperative cooking, floppy physics).
- Catch-up and randomness that keep everyone in the game.

## Pacing shape
Rounds of 1–5 minutes; variety between rounds; an escalating finale (double points, final
round). Sessions are 20–90 minutes and set by the group's energy. Downtime between rounds is
for talking, so it's part of the fun.

## Progression model
Minimal within a session (scores). Across sessions: unlocks, new packs, cosmetics. The real
progression is shared stories and in-jokes.

## Balancing levers
- Skill vs. luck mix (more luck brings in non-gamers; more skill satisfies competitive groups).
- Round length and rule complexity.
- Catch-up strength (keeps the last-placed player engaged).
- Player-count scaling (fun at 3 and at 8?).
- Spectator and audience participation.

## Failure modes
- **Rules that take too long to explain:** the group loses energy.
- **Player elimination** that leaves people watching for long.
- **One expert dominates**, so others disengage.
- **Pressure to perform** that excludes shy players. Offer low-stakes roles.
- **Online party games without moderation**, which leads to toxicity.

## Reference games (and why they work)
| Game | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Jackbox Party Packs | Phones as controllers remove the hardware barrier; humor is generated by the players |
| Overcooked | Cooperative chaos; communication under pressure *is* the game |
| Among Us | Social deduction with simple tasks; accusation and lying produce the fun |
| Mario Party | Luck and minigames level the field for mixed-skill families |
| Journey | Anonymous, wordless co-op creates connection without text chat |

## Design questions
1. Can a non-gamer play round one after a 15-second explanation?
2. What do players say to each other during a round?
3. What does the player in last place do, and do they still have fun?
4. Does it work at the smallest and the largest supported group size?
