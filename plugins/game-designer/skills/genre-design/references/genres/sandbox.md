# Sandbox

Open-ended play spaces where players set their own goals: building, physics toys, systemic
open worlds, creative modes and user-generated content platforms.

## Core loop
Notice a possibility → try it (build, combine, break) → the system responds in an
interesting way → share or show it off → raise your own ambition.

## Signature mechanics
- Composable building blocks (voxels, parts, physics objects, logic components).
- Systemic interactions (fire spreads, water flows, circuits conduct).
- Few hard goals, with optional scaffolding (challenges, achievements, scenarios).
- Sharing and UGC tools: screenshots, blueprints, workshops.
- Save, undo, copy and paste for large creations.

## Pacing shape
Driven by the player. The designer shapes it with **discovery cadence**: how often a new
part, interaction or idea becomes available. Social sharing provides peaks: a finished
build, a viral clip. The risk is the blank-canvas problem at the start, and running out of
ideas later.

## Progression model
Mostly mastery and ambition (bigger, cleverer creations). Optional unlocks give early
structure. Community content becomes the long-term progression for many players.

## Balancing levers
- Starter constraints: a small palette at first, which helps creativity.
- Interaction density: how many systems affect each other.
- The strength of goal scaffolding, toggleable per mode.
- Performance budget (part counts, physics objects).
- How easy sharing is.

## Failure modes
- **Blank-canvas paralysis:** no prompts, no examples, no small first goal.
- **Shallow systems:** combinations don't produce surprises, so there's nothing to discover.
- **Tool friction:** building is slow or clumsy, so creativity stalls.
- **No audience:** no way to show creations, which kills a major motivation.
- **Griefing** in shared sandboxes without protections (claims, rollback, permissions).

## Reference games (and why they work)
| Game | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Minecraft | Simple blocks, systemic redstone, and survival as optional structure scale from toddlers to engineers |
| Garry's Mod | Physics plus tools plus community modes; players invented the games |
| The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild / Tears of the Kingdom | "Multiplicative" systemic rules in an open world reward experimentation |
| Townscaper | Every click produces something pleasing, so there's no blank-canvas problem |

## Design questions
1. What can a player make in the first 5 minutes that they're proud of?
2. Which two systems interact in a surprising way, and how will players find out?
3. What is the "show-off" moment, and how does it get shared?
4. What stops one player from ruining another's creation (if shared)?
