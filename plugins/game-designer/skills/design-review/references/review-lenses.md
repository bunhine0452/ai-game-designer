# Review lenses

Original guiding questions, grouped into 12 lenses. Each is grounded in published
frameworks (see Sources). They are *not* reproductions of any book's lens cards.

## 1. Experience and aesthetics
- What should the player *feel*, and which two or three MDA aesthetics are primary?
- Does each core mechanic produce a dynamic that leads to those aesthetics?
- Which of Lazzaro's four keys (hard, easy, people, serious fun) does the game deliver?
- **Red flags:** aesthetics never stated; features that serve none of the targets.

## 2. Core loop and decisions
- Is the moment-to-moment verb enjoyable with no rewards attached?
- Does each loop feed the next one up, and change the player's options?
- In a typical minute, what decisions does the player make? Are they interesting (close in
  value, readable, consequential)?
- **Red flags:** a dominant choice; blind choices; rewards that are only bigger numbers.

## 3. Pacing and flow
- Does the session have a hook, rising peaks with relief, and a late climax?
- Where might challenge outrun skill (anxiety) or skill outrun challenge (boredom)?
- Do players choose their challenge, or is it imposed?
- **Red flags:** long flat stretches; sustained peaks; an early climax; spikes with no teaching.

## 4. Motivation and needs
- Which needs does the design support: autonomy, competence, relatedness?
- Which motivations does the audience have (Quantic Foundry), and does the core loop serve
  the top two?
- Are extrinsic rewards supporting intrinsic reasons to play, or replacing them?
- **Red flags:** obligation loops; reliance on extrinsic rewards alone; social pressure.

## 5. Onboarding and learnability
- What does the player learn in the first 5 minutes, in what order, and how (doing vs. reading)?
- Is each new mechanic introduced safely, developed, twisted, then combined?
- Can the player recover after a long break (reminders, journals)?
- **Red flags:** front-loaded text tutorials; several mechanics introduced at once; untaught skill tests.

## 6. Feel and feedback
- Does every input get immediate, proportional feedback?
- Do the forgiveness windows (buffering, coyote time, hitbox leniency) suit the genre?
- Do the strongest effects belong to the most important moments?
- **Red flags:** input lag; uniform feedback; juice that hides gameplay information.

## 7. Balance and viable options
- How many options are viable at the top level and at the median?
- Which strategy will optimizers find first, and is it fun to repeat?
- Is randomness mostly input (planned around) or output (luck), and is bad luck capped?
- **Red flags:** a dominant option; untested combinations; output randomness deciding outcomes.

## 8. Progression and economy
- What does the player feel in time per level or upgrade? Is it known?
- Does every faucet have a matching sink that scales with progression?
- Does progression add options (horizontal) as well as power (vertical)?
- **Red flags:** inflation; grind walls; progression that only adds numbers.

## 9. Readability, art and audio
- Can every gameplay-critical element be read at gameplay speed (value, silhouette, motion)?
- Are gameplay colors and sounds reserved for gameplay?
- Does audio carry the information the player needs (wind-ups, direction, ally vs. enemy)?
- **Red flags:** decoration competing with threats; similar silhouettes; color-only signals.

## 10. Accessibility
- Is each critical piece of information carried on two or more channels?
- Are controls remappable, without simultaneous or rapid presses required?
- Are text size, subtitles, motion and flashing adjustable before play starts?
- **Red flags:** accessibility postponed to "later"; difficulty used as the only accessibility tool.

## 11. Scope and feasibility
- Does the content count fit the team's capacity (with integration factors and buffers)?
- Is hidden scope accounted for (save, settings, localization, certification, marketing)?
- What is the smallest version that still delivers the pillars?
- **Red flags:** a genre's appetite mismatched with team size; no vertical slice.

## 12. Ethics and player respect
- Does any mechanic work *against* the player's interests without their informed consent?
- Are real-money prices and odds clear before purchase? Are minors protected?
- Would the team be comfortable explaining each retention mechanic to players openly?
- **Red flags:** see `ethics-dark-patterns.md`.
